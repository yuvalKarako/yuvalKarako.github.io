THE COMMON THREAD

HOW I WORK

Knowledge is the asset,
not the model.

A language model is a brilliant stranger. It has read everything, and knows nothing about you.

Give it your knowledge and it becomes something else: a colleague who was in every meeting, read every paper you read, and remembers the note you wrote in the margin years ago. That is the shift I care about. Not smarter models, but models that work from what a person or an organization already knows.

The architecture is deliberately simple. Plain text, links between ideas, and an index the model maintains itself. Models come and go every few months. This layer compounds.

That makes knowledge an asset in the full sense: something you own, not something a platform holds for you. The files sit on your own machine, readable by any model and any editor, so you can switch tools without losing a thing. Nothing leaves except what you ask a model to read, at the moment you ask. For research data, peer reviews or a grant before submission, that is not a detail.

In the lab, it grounds every diagnosis in the scientific literature. In the classroom, it turns a professor’s decades of notes into something they can talk to. In the IDF, it turns scattered reports into data commanders can trust.
